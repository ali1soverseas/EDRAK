## 2. Overview

GitLab is a publicly traded software company (Nasdaq: GTLB) that sells a single application covering planning, source code management, CI/CD, security scanning, compliance, and deployment. Its handbook describes the company as an open core software company whose DevSecOps platform is used by more than 100,000 organizations. GitLab's investor-relations materials now describe it as "the intelligent orchestration platform for DevSecOps."

The core commercial pitch is **toolchain consolidation**: replace a patchwork of separate developer, security, and operations tools with one system and one data model. Since 2025 that pitch has been extended to AI agents, with GitLab positioning itself as the governed place where humans and agents build software together.

## 3. Mission, Values, and Culture (from the Handbook)

**Mission.** Enable everyone to contribute to and co-create the software that powers our world. Earlier versions of the mission framed this as changing creative work from "read-only" to "read-write."

**Values (CREDIT).** The handbook's values spell out CREDIT, reflecting the idea that team members give each other credit by assuming good intent:

| Letter | Value |
|---|---|
| C | Collaboration |
| R | Results |
| E | Efficiency |
| D | Diversity, Inclusion & Belonging |
| I | Iteration |
| T | Transparency |

**Operating principles**

- **All-remote.** GitLab is one of the world's largest all-remote companies. No team member is required to work from an office, and the company has no real headquarters.
- **Handbook-first.** How the company runs is documented in a public handbook of thousands of pages, covering every department. Anyone, including outsiders, can suggest changes by merge request.
- **Asynchronous by default,** with synchronous meetings used for speed where it matters.
- **Dogfooding.** GitLab runs its own company, including the handbook, on its own product.
- **Transparency.** Strategy, values, and OKRs are public, and much internal communication has historically been shared openly (for example on the GitLab Unfiltered YouTube channel).
- **Culture rituals.** Company-wide Summits and Contribute events, Team Member Resource Groups, and a "Top Team Member" recognition.

> **Handbook status note:** The 2026 "Act 2" strategy introduced new core beliefs (see §9) that emphasize speed, fewer handoffs, and agent automation. Some community members have questioned how this fits the historic open-book culture. Check the live handbook for the current wording.

## 6. Products and Platform

### 6.1 Tiers

| Tier | Positioning | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | Small teams and open source | Limited to 5 users per top-level group on GitLab.com; 400 compute minutes. No included AI credits, but credits can now be purchased as an add-on. |
| **Premium** | Team productivity and collaboration | Listed around $29 per user per month, billed annually. Includes Duo Core and a bundled monthly credit allowance (see §7). |
| **Ultimate** | Enterprise security, compliance, planning | Custom pricing. Adds advanced security scanning, compliance, and portfolio management. Larger bundled credit allowance. |

### 6.2 Deployment options

- **GitLab.com (SaaS)** — multi-tenant, GitLab-managed.
- **GitLab Self-Managed** — customer-hosted, including air-gapped environments.
- **GitLab Dedicated** — single-tenant SaaS managed by GitLab, aimed at regulated customers. GitLab cited Dedicated and Duo as drivers of SaaS growth in Q2 FY2027.

### 6.3 Capability areas

Planning and agile portfolio management, source code management, CI/CD pipelines and runners, security testing (SAST, DAST, dependency and secret scanning), compliance and audit, package and container registries, deployment, and a Secrets Manager add-on (launched August 2026 for Premium and Ultimate on GitLab.com).

### 6.4 Newer offerings

- **GitLab Duo Agent Platform** — agentic AI across the lifecycle (see §7).
- **GitLab Orbit** — a 2026 offering introduced alongside the Flex Commitment pricing change. Public detail was limited at the time of writing, so verify against GitLab's own documentation before relying on it.
- **Managed Service Provider program** — expanded in February 2026 to meet agentic AI demand.

## 7. AI Strategy: Duo, AI Gateway, and Credits

### 7.1 Evolution

1. **GitLab Duo (2023–2024)** — code suggestions, chat, and AI assistants, sold partly as Duo Pro and Duo Enterprise add-ons.
2. **Duo Core (GitLab 18.0)** — baseline Code Suggestions and Agentic Chat included at no extra charge in Premium and Ultimate.
3. **Duo Agent Platform (GA January 15, 2026)** — agents and flows that act across planning, coding, review, security, and delivery, billed by consumption.

### 7.2 AI Gateway and model neutrality

- The **AI Gateway** is the service that routes Duo requests to large language models.
- By default, Duo uses the GitLab-hosted AI Gateway with GitLab-selected external model vendors.
- With **GitLab Duo Self-Hosted**, customers run their own AI Gateway and their own models. The documentation says request and response logs then stay in the customer's domain, with no external API calls required, which supports offline and air-gapped use.
- Self-hosted setups can point at any OpenAI-API-compatible model endpoint. GitLab's supported-models list includes Anthropic Claude models, GLM models, and GPT-family models, among others.
- A caveat in the documentation: any feature left on a GitLab-managed model still routes through GitLab's hosted gateway, so full isolation requires assigning a self-hosted model to every feature in use.
- GitLab's AI Transparency Center states that GitLab does not currently train its own models, and it lists the subprocessors involved.

### 7.3 GitLab Credits

- Usage-based virtual currency at about **$1 per credit** on demand.
- Premium and Ultimate include **$12** and **$24** of credits per user per month respectively. GitLab's pricing page describes these allowances as promotional and subject to change.
- Customers can also buy a Monthly Commitment Pool. On-demand usage on Free namespaces is capped at $25,000 per calendar month.
- Hybrid pricing (seats plus consumption) and the **Flex** commitment model are central to GitLab's growth plan. On the Q2 FY2027 call, management said Duo Agent Platform paid consumption grew about 50% sequentially, and that moving self-managed renewals to Flex shifts some revenue timing (up to roughly $13M of FY2027 revenue).

## 9. Strategy: "GitLab Act 2" (May 2026)

CEO Bill Staples published "GitLab Act 2" as a letter to customers and investors. Its central claim is that GitLab can become the trusted enterprise platform for software creation in the AI era.

**Core beliefs (selected, paraphrased from a list of ten)**

- Software will increasingly be built by machines and directed by people, and cheaper software production will expand demand for software.
- Agents should be first-class users of the platform, with agent-specific APIs.
- Governance (identity, audit, policy, deployment flexibility) should be a core platform service that every agent, pipeline, and merge request passes through.
- Enterprises will live across human-owned, agent-assisted, and agent-autonomous work, and GitLab aims to serve all three on one platform, one data model, and one governance system.
- The platform stays cloud-neutral and AI-model-neutral.

**Restructuring**

- Flatten management by up to three layers in some functions (about eight layers deep before).
- Reorganize R&D into roughly 60 autonomous teams.
- Reduce country footprint by about 30% (reported as 22 countries) and cut around 350 roles, roughly 14% of the workforce, after a voluntary separation window.
- Automate internal reviews, approvals, and handoffs with AI agents.
- Leadership stated the move was not a cost-cutting exercise and that most savings would be reinvested. Shares fell 7–8% in after-hours trading when it was announced.

| Planning and workflow suites | Atlassian (Jira, Bitbucket) | Unified data model rather than integrated separate products. |

## 11. Strengths and Risks

**Strengths**

- Single platform and data model across planning, code, CI/CD, security, and compliance.
- Strong enterprise footprint: GitLab states that more than half of the Fortune 100 use it.
- Flexible deployment (SaaS, self-managed, dedicated, air-gapped) and model-neutral AI with a self-hosted option.
- Consistent profitability on a non-GAAP basis and strong cash generation.
- Distinctive transparency-driven culture and public handbook.

**Risks and open questions**

- Execution risk from simultaneous restructuring, platform re-architecture, and pricing changes.
- Revenue growth has slowed from 26% (FY2026) to 21% (Q2 FY2027), and Q3 guidance implies a sequential dip tied partly to Flex timing effects.
- Heavy competition from Microsoft/GitHub, Atlassian, and fast-moving AI-native tools.
- Seat-based revenue could be pressured if agents reduce developer headcount, though GitLab is betting credits offset this.
- Pricing complexity has increased, and published prices have changed repeatedly in 2026.
- Cultural questions after Act 2, including community concern over the open-source emphasis.

## 12. Data Confidence and Caveats

- **Financials and customer counts** come from GitLab's SEC filings and press releases, which are the most reliable sources here.
- **Pricing** varies by source. Premium is widely listed at $29 per user per month, but some trackers report GitLab now shows "contact us" for Premium. Ultimate is described as custom, though one third-party site lists $99 per user per month. Confirm on GitLab's pricing page before quoting.
- **Workforce size** is not stated directly in the sources reviewed. The roughly 350 cut roles equal about 14%, which implies a prior headcount near 2,500, but treat that as an inference.
- **Handbook size** is reported inconsistently (2,000 to 8,000+ pages) across sources, so this profile says "thousands of pages."
- **GitLab Orbit** and the Flex Commitment have limited detail in the sources reviewed.
- Third-party summaries (blogs, aggregators) were used for color only. Where possible, facts were anchored to GitLab or SEC documents.
