## Offer and customer context

### Products and services in scope

NVIDIA's public data-center materials describe a portfolio involving accelerated compute, systems, networking, and software. Relevant company-profile categories are:

- **AI computing:** accelerators and associated computing platforms used for AI and other accelerated workloads.
- **Data-center platforms:** integrated compute, system, networking, and software elements that customers deploy as a service or within their own facilities.
- **Developer ecosystem:** software, libraries, tools, runtimes, and model workflows that influence developer access and porting effort.
- **Cloud access:** services and partner offerings through which customers may consume compute. Confirm each service, provider, country, instance, and available capacity separately.

Use the current NVIDIA product catalogue, documentation, and filings to confirm product names, versions, release status, technical characteristics, and region-specific availability before using them in a decision.

### Target buyers

**Cloud service providers (CSPs)** may evaluate a territory based on customer demand, feasible data-center capacity, grid and power access, cooling, networking, delivery lead times, financing, service operations, and the ability to offer reliable AI instances or managed services.

**AI developers** may evaluate access to compute, compatible frameworks and models, setup time, software support, cloud region, data handling requirements, performance for their actual workload, and the cost and operational effort of deployment.

For each geography, distinguish infrastructure owner/operator, cloud provider, AI platform team, developer, procurement team, facilities/energy team, and end customer. They can have different buying authority and different readiness criteria.

## Geography scope: United States and Europe

### United States

The company profile identifies the United States as a current market. Analyze it as a collection of local markets, not as one uniform deployment environment. Customer location, state and local permitting, utility territory, grid interconnection, facility readiness, tax and incentive conditions, procurement, service coverage, and export-control rules can differ by project and product.

For an AI-compute or data-center opportunity, validate:

- named customer and use case, workload demand, buying stage, and funding evidence;
- existing and planned compute capacity, confirmed region/zone, capacity reservation, and service level;
- available power, cooling design, network connectivity, construction schedule, and local approvals;
- supply lead times and eligibility for the exact product configuration and destination;
- commercial terms and total operating costs over a defined utilization and time horizon;
- applicable federal, state, and local requirements with qualified legal/compliance review.

Do not use a general national AI-demand estimate as evidence that a particular state, metro area, customer, or data center has deployable capacity.

### Europe

The profile identifies Europe as a current market. **Europe is not one legal, electricity, procurement, or cloud-availability jurisdiction.** For every finding, name the country (and where necessary the EU/EEA or non-EU distinction), service region, customer type, and effective date.

Validate:

- country-specific data-center, cloud, customer, and partner availability;
- power availability, grid connection queue, energy cost, water/cooling constraints, network connectivity, and facility delivery schedule;
- data protection, cybersecurity, data location, procurement, and sector-specific requirements applicable to the customer and service;
- applicable EU rules and their scope; do not assume a rule applies identically to every hardware vendor, cloud service, developer, or AI system;
- local language, support coverage, distributor/system integrator capability, and deployment skills;
- product/export authorization and destination eligibility for the exact product and end user.

The EU AI Act is a risk-based framework for AI systems; the applicable duties depend on the actor, system, and use. Do not claim that a data-center accelerator itself is automatically classified as a high-risk AI system. Determine whether NVIDIA, a cloud provider, customer, or developer is acting as provider, deployer, or another regulated role for the specific service/use, then seek qualified legal review.

### Infrastructure and energy readiness

AI compute market attractiveness depends on more than the number of potential users. Data-center power, grid access, cooling, networking, construction, capacity, and operational capability can constrain when or whether demand can be served. The International Energy Agency's Energy and AI work is a useful external source for macro-level energy/AI context; it is not a substitute for local utility, site, and project evidence.

Separate:

1. **Demand signal:** customer statement, procurement, contract, pilot, or deployed workload.
2. **Infrastructure readiness:** confirmed power, facility, cooling, network, and schedule.
3. **Commercial feasibility:** cost, utilization, customer economics, and support obligations.
4. **Regulatory readiness:** applicable laws and approvals confirmed for the actual jurisdiction and actor.
5. **NVIDIA-specific feasibility:** exact product eligibility, supply, qualified system, cloud region, and support.

Do not infer local readiness from global market projections or a partner announcement.

## Market-entry and expansion assessment

Use the following dimensions for a country, region, customer segment, or channel. Score each only after defining the evidence and rating scale.

### 1. Customer demand

- Which CSPs or AI developer segments have the need?
- Is demand for training, inference, fine-tuning, AI services, or another workload?
- Is the evidence an interview, RFP, budget, paid pilot, purchase, or production deployment?
- What capacity, latency, availability, data-handling, and support requirements are documented?
- How many independent customers support the conclusion? Are they representative?

### 2. Route to market

- Direct sale, cloud service, distributor, OEM/system integrator, reseller, or partner-led delivery?
- Who owns the end-customer relationship, support, uptime, maintenance, and incident response?
- Are local installation, qualification, financing, and technical skills available?
- Are partners contracted, publicly announced, merely potential, or not yet identified?

### 3. Infrastructure feasibility

- What compute, rack, networking, storage, and software configuration is required?
- Is site power confirmed, contracted, and available on the required timeline?
- Are cooling, water, building, grid interconnect, fiber, and physical security sufficient?
- Can the site sustain the target utilization and service-level objective?
- What are the lead time, dependencies, bottlenecks, and responsible owners?

### 4. Regulatory, security, and trade controls

- Which country and customer sector are in scope?
- What data-protection, cybersecurity, AI-system, procurement, export-control, and sanctions rules apply?
- Which entity is responsible for each obligation: hardware provider, cloud provider, deployer, developer, or customer?
- What records or approvals establish the product, destination, end-user, and use are permitted?
- Which interpretations require legal/compliance confirmation rather than an analyst assumption?

### 5. Economics and risks

Build conservative, base, and upside scenarios with explicit assumptions. Include acquisition/lease cost, compute utilization, energy, cooling, networking, storage, deployment, support, software, financing, service revenue, and delay risk where relevant. Clearly label customer-specific economics, public pricing, analyst estimates, and unverified assumptions. Do not present an addressable-market estimate as NVIDIA revenue or guaranteed demand.

### 6. Competitive alternatives

For the profile's tracked competitors, investigate **Broadcom** alternatives (including custom accelerator and networking cases where documented) and **Intel Gaudi** alternatives. Establish whether each is genuinely available to the target customer, in the target country, for the same workload. Include other substitutes only when the research task calls for them; do not expand the scope without reason.

## Evidence standard and decision outputs

For each claim, capture source, date, country, product/version, measurement basis, claim type, confidence, and limitations. Prefer primary regulatory text and regulator guidance for compliance; local utility or project sources for infrastructure; public filings and official customer disclosures for corporate claims; and named customer evidence for deployments.

Keep evidence types separate:

- **Observed:** deployed or measured result with traceable evidence.
- **Committed:** signed order, contract, or formally approved capacity.
- **Announced:** public statement or future plan not yet observed.
- **Estimated:** forecast or model with stated methodology.
- **Hypothesis:** open assumption to validate.

Recommended output: a country-by-country readiness matrix with demand strength, accessible capacity, infrastructure readiness, route to market, compliance questions, competitive options, scenario economics, critical dependencies, confidence, and a **go / validate / defer** set of options. The final investment decision belongs to authorized people.
